# Knowledge: Brownie Science

Product-specific knowledge for Brownies (and, loosely, other dense fat/sugar-heavy baked goods
like Revel Bars). Referenced by `specialists/food-science-quality-specialist.md` and
`specialists/product-development-chef.md` when the product under review is Brownies. Read
alongside `food-science.md` for the general food-safety/shelf-life fundamentals this specializes.

## Texture chemistry

- Fudgy vs. cakey texture is mainly a **fat-to-flour and egg-to-flour ratio** question — more
  fat and egg relative to flour gives a denser, fudgier result; more flour and leavening gives a
  cakier one. A texture complaint in tasting feedback often traces back to one of these ratios,
  not the bake time.
- **Sugar is a humectant**, not just a sweetener — it binds water and slows moisture loss, which
  is a real factor in how long a brownie stays soft. A lower-sugar reformulation (e.g. for a
  "less sweet" tasting request) can shorten how long the product stays fresh-textured, not just
  change the taste — flag this as a real trade-off when a sugar reduction is being tested, not
  just a flavor change.
- **Cocoa powder vs. chocolate compound/bar** behave differently in a formula: cocoa powder adds
  dry cocoa solids without extra fat, while a chocolate compound/bar adds both cocoa solids and
  fat — swapping the ratio between them changes both texture and moisture retention, not just
  chocolate intensity.

## Staling and shelf-life implications

- A high-butter, high-chocolate-compound fudgy brownie (like a formula heavy in butter and
  chocolate) is generally **moisture- and fat-rich**, which slows staling relative to a leaner
  cakey formula — but it's still a baked good with real starch content, so staling (drying,
  surface toughening) over 24-72 hours at room temperature is a reasonable thing to expect and
  test for, not assume away just because the recipe is "fudgy."
- This is general baking-science guidance, not a measured shelf-life claim for any specific
  formula — an actual timed tasting/texture check (see
  `workflows/product-experiment-design.md`) is what turns this into a real finding.

## Packaging implications specific to this product

A dense, moist brownie has different packaging needs than a crisp cookie — a sealed environment
that traps excess surface moisture can cause sogginess or mold risk just as easily as an
unsealed one can dry it out. See `packaging.md` for the general packaging-science side of this;
the two should be reviewed together, not separately.
