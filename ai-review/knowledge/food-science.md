# Knowledge: Food Science Fundamentals

Referenced by `specialists/food-science-quality-specialist.md`. General food-science and
food-safety grounding — read the relevant product-specific file too (`brownie-science.md`,
`coffee-extraction.md`) and `packaging.md` when they apply to the product under review.

## Food safety fundamentals

- **TCS foods** (Time/Temperature Control for Safety — dairy, eggs, cut produce, cooked
  starches) support faster pathogen growth outside safe temperature ranges and need real
  temperature control through storage and delivery, not just at time of baking.
- **Water activity (Aw)** — not the same as moisture content — is the single biggest driver of
  how long a food can sit before microbial growth becomes a real risk. Low-Aw baked goods (most
  cookies, dense brownies) are far more shelf-stable than high-Aw items (custards, cream fillings,
  fresh dairy drinks). When in doubt about a specific product's Aw, treat it as unknown rather
  than assuming "baked = automatically safe for days."
- **Hazard categories** to check for, not just taste/texture: biological (bacteria, mold),
  chemical (allergen cross-contact, cleaning residue), physical (foreign material). A specialist
  review that only checks taste/texture hasn't covered food safety.

## Shelf-life and stability

- **Staling** (starch retrogradation and moisture migration) is a texture/quality issue, distinct
  from a safety issue — a product can be stale but still safe, or fresh-tasting but unsafe if
  temperature-abused. Don't conflate "tastes fine" with "was handled safely."
- Real shelf-life claims come from either **real-time testing** (store it the actual way, taste
  and inspect it on a schedule) or **accelerated testing** (elevated temperature to estimate
  faster) — a claim with neither behind it is an assumption, not a finding, regardless of how
  confident it sounds.
- Packaging changes the shelf-life answer — the same recipe can be shelf-stable for days in one
  packaging format and risky in another. Shelf life and packaging are two different reviews that
  have to agree with each other, not one review covering both by assumption.

## Applying this in a review

Treat any freshness/shelf-life/stability claim in a costing note, packaging line, or product
description as **unverified until a real test is cited**. A packaging cost being nonzero is not
a test. See the relevant specialist's verdict triggers for exactly when this becomes a FAIL or
VETO.
