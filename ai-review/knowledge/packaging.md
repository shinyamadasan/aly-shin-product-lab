# Knowledge: Packaging Science

Referenced by `specialists/food-science-quality-specialist.md` and
`specialists/supply-chain-manager.md`. General packaging fundamentals — read alongside the
relevant product-specific knowledge file (`brownie-science.md`, `coffee-extraction.md`) for how
these interact with a specific product.

## What packaging actually needs to do

Packaging has functional jobs, not just a cost and a look: moisture barrier (keep a baked good
from drying out or, conversely, keep outside moisture from making it soggy), oxygen barrier
(slow staling/rancidity in fat-heavy items), physical protection (crushing, leakage), and — for
bottled drinks — insulation/cold retention and leak-proofing. A packaging line that only accounts
for a box or a bag's PHP cost hasn't accounted for whether it does any of these jobs.

## The core gap this framework exists to catch

**A nonzero packaging cost is not evidence of a tested packaging choice.** Common home-bakery
packaging options — heat-sealed bags, box + liner, vacuum seal, insulated bottle sleeves — have
genuinely different barrier properties, and picking one because it's available or cheap is a
different decision than picking one because it was tested against the product's actual
moisture/oxygen/temperature needs. When reviewing a packaging cost line with no accompanying
test note, treat the packaging choice as **unverified**, not as settled.

## Delivery and transport conditions matter as much as the packaging itself

A packaging choice that works fine sitting on a counter can fail during actual preorder pickup or
delivery — heat exposure in transit, time between packing and pickup, and handling all affect
whether the packaging's barrier properties hold up in practice, not just in a controlled test at
home. A stress test that doesn't reflect real delivery conditions is weaker evidence than one
that does — see `specialists/food-science-quality-specialist.md`'s CONCERN vs. PASS distinction
for this exact situation.

## For Supply Chain Manager use

Packaging materials are a purchased supply with the same "buy in bulk, use per unit" shape as
ingredients — pack size, supplier reliability, and price history apply to a box or bag exactly
as they do to flour or cocoa. Don't treat packaging sourcing as a one-time decision separate from
normal supply management.
