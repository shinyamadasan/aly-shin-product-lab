# Knowledge: Coffee Extraction & Bottled Coffee Stability

Product-specific knowledge for the bottled coffee line (Bottled Caramel Macchiato, Bottled
Spanish Latte). Referenced by `specialists/food-science-quality-specialist.md` and
`specialists/product-development-chef.md` when the product under review is a bottled coffee
drink. Read alongside `food-science.md` for general fundamentals.

## Why `PRODUCT_LAB_CONTEXT.md` singles this category out

Bottled coffee is explicitly flagged as **not assumed premium or fresh enough yet** — it must be
chaos-tested for freshness, separation, cold delivery, packaging, and perceived value before
becoming a hero product. Treat that as a standing instruction whenever this category comes up,
not a one-time note to skim past.

## Extraction basics relevant to a review

- **Cold brew vs. hot-brew-then-chill** produce meaningfully different flavor profiles — cold
  brew is generally lower in acidity and bitterness, hot-brew-then-chill can carry more
  bitterness and can develop off-notes faster once bottled. Which method was used changes what
  "tastes right" even means for this product, and should be part of the tasting record, not
  assumed constant across batches.
- **Extraction strength and dilution** (how concentrated the base coffee is before milk/flavor is
  added) affects both taste and how well the drink holds up to being bottled and chilled for a
  period before drinking — an under-extracted or over-diluted batch degrades differently than a
  well-balanced one.

## Stability risks specific to bottled dairy-coffee drinks

- **Separation** (dairy/fat rising or settling out of the coffee base) is a real, common failure
  mode for bottled milk-coffee drinks, not a rare edge case — this is why a "stir before
  drinking" instruction exists as a real mitigation in `PRODUCT_LAB_CONTEXT.md`'s Launch section,
  not just boilerplate copy.
- **Cold-chain integrity** matters more here than for a baked good — a dairy-based bottled drink
  that goes through a temperature excursion (even briefly, e.g. during delivery) is a genuine
  food-safety concern, not just a quality one. "Extra ice or cold-chain support for drinks" is
  called out in `PRODUCT_LAB_CONTEXT.md` for exactly this reason.
- **Caramel/flavor syrups** can affect both shelf stability and separation behavior differently
  than a plain milk-coffee base — don't assume a syrup-flavored variant behaves the same as an
  unflavored one without testing it separately.

## Applying this in a review

A "premium feel" tasting comment is not the same as a freshness/stability finding — both need to
be evaluated, and neither substitutes for the other. See
`specialists/food-science-quality-specialist.md` for when an unresolved stability question
becomes a VETO for this category specifically.
